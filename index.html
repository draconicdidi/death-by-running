The creed

Hasta la muerte means three things here.

To the workout
All the way.

Nothing left in the tank at the last rep.

To each other
Till the end.

We show up. Nobody gets left on the track.

To death
You're coming anyway.

We'll be running when you get here.

The skeleton on the back of the hoodie isn't a warning. He's you, later, and he's still running. Grinning. Roses on his head, because the ending is what makes it beautiful. The clock is a witness, not a judge. The only question at the end is whether you emptied it.

The chest

Doubloons. Earned, never bought.

Every Friday writes coins into your pile. The pile is your ticket count. When the captain pays the Ferryman, one name crosses over and takes the prize; their pile goes back into the river, and everyone else keeps stacking.

The crossing

Pay the Ferryman.

Every so often, after the workout, the captain opens the chest. Every doubloon in it is one ticket. The Ferryman calls one name. That runner crosses over with the hoodie, and their pile goes back to the river. The rest of us keep stacking, which is to say: keep showing up.

The whole ledger is public. Who's aboard, who's holding tickets, who crossed. Open it on your phone; it remembers you after the first scan.
